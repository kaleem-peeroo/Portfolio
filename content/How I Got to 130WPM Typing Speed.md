That is a screenshot of my fastest typing speed recorded on my [MonkeyType profile.](https://monkeytype.com/profile/DrBlitzer) That is a speed of 131 words per minute (WPM). For context, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_per_minute), "professional typists type at speeds of 43 to 80 wpm". Table 1 of [this paper](https://www.asarif.com/pub/Arif_TIC-STH2009.pdf) shows an average WPM of 65 and 87 for 2 separate studies looking at typing speeds. It's safe to say that my typing speed is well above the average typist. So how did I get here?

It starts many years ago. I was in my early teens, I think 13 or 14, and I wanted to be a cool hacker. The type you would see in movies who could type real fast on a keyboard and hack into the system mainframe in seconds. So I wanted to learn how to type fast and after browing a few Reddit posts I came across the concept of touch typing. Up to that point, I and most people, type by pecking at the keyboard with 1 or 2 fingers and we got quite good at it which did make us faster. Touch typing introduces the concept of using all 8 fingers (not counting thumbs of course) to type instead of just 1 or 2.

There are now free websites out there that help teach you how to touch type by giving you typing drills. These systems enforce the use of all fingers for specific fingers which build up your muscle memory. The one I use these days is [keybr.com](https://www.keybr.com/). It'll start you off with (what seems like) random combinations of letters which get you more comfortable with the finger placements. After a lot of practice you can then move on to words. I personally use keybr with words and here is the daily routine I use to practice my typing to improve it.

![[My WPM Distribution from monkeytype.png]]
_My distribution of typing speeds as recorded via MonkeyType before and after practicing on keybr._

I start by "warming up" with some [MonkeyType](https://monkeytype.com/) 30-second tests. I usually do about 3. The first is just a warm up, the second is where I slow down and aim to get 100%, and the third is where I speed up while keeping accuracy as high as I can. After these 3 runs I then start on keybr. I make sure to do at least 10 minutes of keybr everyday. I then follow this up with a few more runs of MonkeyType. Over the months and after reading many Reddit posts and various articles, I have configured my keybr settings to maximise typing improvement/learning. Here is a breakdown of the settings that are not default and why:

![[my top typing speed from keybr.png]]
_My top typing speed according to keybr is 127.6wpm and beats 99.84% of all other people._

**Target Typing Speed: 100wpm + "unlock next key only when the previous keys are also above the target speed"**
I aim to type more than 100wpm so I set the target typing speed at 100wpm. This means that keybr does not proceed to the next letter until I hit 100wpm which forces you to drill a specific letter. In most cases, you drill it enough and it reaches 100wpm. Funnily enough, I've noticed that keybr sometimes does not stricly enforce this. I've noticed cases where it proceeds to another letter despite being under 100wpm. Nonetheless, the variation ensures that I don't get rusty on letters that I haven't worked on in a while.

**Prefer Natural Words**
I have reached a point where I am comfortable with the finger placements and touch typing technique and can therefore practice using natural words as the whole point of learning to type faster is to type - which usually involves typing natural words...

**Repeate Each Word: 2**
By repeating each word twice, you reinforce the muscle memory because you are typing the word again, regardless of if you typed it incorrectly or not on the first instance of the word. This is especially good in the case where you make a mistake in the first instance of the word because you get a second chance to retype the word correctly which further reinforces the correct muscle memory.

**Add Words to Lessons: About 40%**

- do at least 10 mins keybr everyday
- test on monkey type before and after practice
- slow down
- Aim for 100% accuracy, not high speed
- when you get a word wrong - delete the whole word and rewrite it using ctrl+backspace or option+backspace
- if you miss a day just continue the next day
- I used to practice this many years ago but recently I've realised that my typing has gotten worse. Slower and more mistakes which makes it even slower.
