When I first started my PhD I had no idea how to actually read a paper. I would spend hours reading and re-reading papers and making notes and trying to highlight main points and trying to draw the main contributions from a paper. Some of these papers that I would spend hours reading and understanding were sometimes vaguely of not relevant to my work. I realised (just like in other areas of my work and life) my current system was not the best.

I use the 3 pass method from [[1_main/How to Read a Paper|How to Read a Paper]]. In it's essence you do the following 3 passes where each pass builds upon the last:
- **Pass 1**
	- 5 to 10 mins
	- read the 
		- title
		- abstract
		- intro
		- headings and subheadings
		- conclusion
- **Pass 2**
	- up to 1 hour
	- rest the entire paper while skipping details like proofs and algorithms
- **Pass 3**
	- up to 6 hours
	- virtually recreate the paper

# Pass 1
⏱ 5 to 10 minutes

Read the title, abstract, intro, headings + subheadings, and conclusion.
At the end of the first pass you have a vague idea of what the paper is about. 

I usually aim to answer the following questions after pass 1:
- Category: What kind of paper is this? Novel system? Evaluation of existing methods? Etc.
- Clarity: Was it easy to read?
- Context: What existing methods/techniques did this paper use?
- Contributions: What are the main contributions of this paper?
- Correctness: Are the mentioned assumptions valid?

With this info you can decide whether its worth reading further or to be discarded. If good to continue reading then you do pass 2.

# Pass 2
⏱ up to 1 hour

This is the point where you read the entire paper from start to finish. Ignore the details e.g. proofs and algorithms. Write notes. Make annotations. Write comments. Question things.

At the end of pass 2 you should be able to summarise the work to a colleague.
# Pass 3
⏱ up to 6 hours...

This pass takes the longest because you will be virtually recreating the work. You present yourself wth the same problem, same context, and the same assumptions, and then you basically create your own solution as if you were doing the work of the paper before the paper was written.
Find and challenge every assumption.
Write down ideas for future work.
You then compare your recreation with the paper.

At the end of this pass you shoud be able to:
- recreate the entire paper from memory
- find strengths and weaknesses of the paper
- find hidden assumptions
- find missing citations to relevant work
- find potential issues with experiments or analytical techniques

And that is how I read papers using 3 passes. I hope you learnt something new today - if not, feel free to share how you read papers. You can email me at mail@KaleemPeeroo.com.
