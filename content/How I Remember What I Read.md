For a long time I would read non-fiction books and a few months later I would remember maybe 2 or 3 things from what I read. It seemed like a waste of time. All that time spent reading and not remembering everything I wanted to remember. That system was not working. I couldn't rely on my brain to absorb and store 100% of what I read. Even 50% of what I read. I had to come up with a new system. 

I searched for the most effective methods to remember things and found 2 keys things: flash cards and spaced repetition. These were the fundamental blocks for my new system. I've used flash cards and spaced repetition before, during my undergrad to study various computer science topics. It worked well back then so I assumed it would work well now. So I wanted to find a way to create this new system. 

My current system for reading at the time would let me highlight content from books which would be stored in Obsidian. I'd then write my own notes of my highlights. Now all I had to do is create flash cards and use spaced repetition. Back in undergrad, I used Anki for that. Luckily Obsidian has a plugin called spaced repetition which lets you create flashcards within your notes and use spaced repetition to schedule when to review them. You even have the option to hide content in your notes using closures to act as flash cards. So I started using it which meant going through my notes and creating flash cards from them which took some time. 

I broke it down and created a task in my daily note template to add or improve at least 1 flash card a day. It's the same system I've used to write blog posts or do anything big - I just do a little chunk everyday. In the case of writing blog posts I have a task to write at least 1 sentence a day. Including this post that you're reading right now. Chances are this sentence was written on a different day as the first sentence of this post. In fact, this very sentence that you are reading at the moment was written the day after the previous sentence. This system lets me slowly add flash cards day after day. The great thing about spaced repetition is that over time I review less and less cards.

I reached a point where I had managed to successfully create flashcards from my existing content. Everyday I would spend 5 to 10 minutes reviewing flashcards. That's the key to this whole system. You. Reviewing flashcards. Everyday. It's the little thing that you do everyday that compounds over time. You review the flashcards for a few minutes everyday and after months you'll be remembering facts like you're taking that miracle drug from the movie Limitless.

As time goes on I would read new books, annotate them and then create flash cards for the annotations in Obsidian. To this day, I continue reading, annotating, creating flash cards, and reviewing them every day. If you're interested in trying it out let me know how you get along.
