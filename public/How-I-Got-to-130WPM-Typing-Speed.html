How I Got to 130WPM Typing Speed

How I Got to 130WPM Typing Speed

Jun 23, 20267 min read

That is a screenshot of my fastest typing speed recorded on my MonkeyType profile. That is a speed of 131 words per minute (WPM). But its just a single data point…and with my background, I appreciate distributions and the nature of being statistical, so below is my entire distribution of typing speeds.


My distribution of typing speeds as recorded via MonkeyType before and after practicing on keybr.

For context, according to Wikipedia, “professional typists type at speeds of 43 to 80 wpm”. Table 1 of this paper shows an average WPM of 65 and 87 for 2 separate studies looking at typing speeds. It’s safe to say that my typing speed is above the average typist.
Is it really though?

…

I don’t think so.
MonkeyType tests your typing speed with the most common and easy to type words which means the typing speed measured is likely inflated. Below, is my top typing speed according to keybr, a total of 128 WPM and beats 99.84% of all other people (at least other people being keybr’s other users). Why is keybr’s WPM a better measure than MonkeyType? Because it tests you on any English word and therefore more similarly replicates the paper mentioned. So, how did I get here?


My top typing speed according to keybr is 127.6wpm and beats 99.84% of all other people.

It started many years ago. I was in my early teens, I think 13 or 14, and I wanted to be a cool hacker. The type you would see in movies who could type real fast on a keyboard and hack into the system mainframe in seconds. So I wanted to learn how to type fast and after browing a few Reddit posts I came across the concept of touch typing. Up to that point, I, and most people, type by pecking at the keyboard with 1 or 2 fingers and we got quite good at it which did make us faster…but it could be even faster. Touch typing introduces the concept of using all 8 fingers (not counting thumbs of course) to type instead of just 1 or 2.

There are now many free websites out there that help teach you how to touch type by giving you typing drills. These systems enforce the use of all fingers for specific fingers which build up your muscle memory. The one I use these days is keybr.com. It’ll start you off with (what seems like) random combinations of letters which get you more comfortable with the finger placements. After a lot of practice you can then move on to words. I personally use keybr with words and here is the daily routine I use to practice my typing to improve it.

I start by “warming up” with some MonkeyType 30-second tests. I usually do about 3. The first is just a warm up, the second is where I slow down and aim to get 100%, and the third is where I speed up while keeping accuracy as high as I can. After these 3 runs I then start on keybr. I do a typing test on keybr and then practice and then do another typing test on keybr after the practice. I make sure to do at least 10 minutes of keybr everyday.

The question is…why do all this work? Is it really worth it to type faster? Does it actually improve your typing speed? Here is a graph showing various stats from my typing lessons over time. Purple is the number of keys per lesson, orange/brown is my accuracy and green is my typing speed.

Horizontal axis: lesson number. Vertical axis: typing speed (green), accuracy (orange/brown), number of keys in lesson (purple).

Take a good look at it.

Seriously. Go back and look.

What do you notice?

Here is what I notice. The graph can be split horizontally into a left side and right side around the 700th lesson where the green and brown “lines” cross over.
On the left, my speed was higher but my accuracy was lower which tells us that if I prioritise speed over accuracy I am much more inaccurate. One noticeable thing from the graph is how the gap between speed and accuracy increases before the switching point. Yes, the green points rise quite high, BUT, the orange points sink lower…
On the other hand, on the right side, both the typing speed in green and the accuracy in orange/brown slowly increase over the lessons over time and this signifies that my speed will get faster over time if I continue to prioritise accuracy.

So this system does work. Doing 15 minutes of typing practice a day (10 minutes practice + typing tests) everyday improves both your speed and accuracy - at least in my case. Of course, this assumes you prioritise accuracy over speed when you are practicing. Over the months and after reading many Reddit posts and various articles, I have configured my keybr settings to maximise typing improvement/learning. Here is a breakdown of the settings that are not the default and why:

Target Typing Speed: 100wpm + “unlock next key only when the previous keys are also above the target speed”
I aim to type more than 100wpm so I set the target typing speed at 100wpm. This means that keybr does not proceed to the next letter until I hit 100wpm which forces you to drill a specific letter. In most cases, you drill it enough and it reaches 100wpm. Funnily enough, I’ve noticed that keybr sometimes does not stricly enforce this. I’ve noticed cases where it proceeds to another letter despite being under 100wpm. Nonetheless, the variation ensures that I don’t get rusty on letters that I haven’t worked on in a while.

Prefer Natural Words
I have reached a point where I am comfortable with the finger placements and touch typing technique and can therefore practice using natural words as the whole point of learning to type faster is to type - which usually involves typing natural words…

Repeat Each Word: 2
By repeating each word twice, I reinforce the muscle memory because I am typing the word again, regardless of if I typed it incorrectly or not on the first instance of the word. This is especially good in the case where you make a mistake in the first instance of the word because you get a second chance to retype the word correctly which further reinforces the correct muscle memory.

Add Words to Lessons: About 40%
In my experimentation, I found this to be the sweet spot of number of words where it was long enough to aim for 100% accuracy but short enough to forgive inaccuracies. What does that mean? When I tried longer lessons, I found myself more likely to make at least one mistake and when lessons were shorter I found the opposite, I was more likely to make no mistakes. 40% was a good balance to challenge me to aim for 100% accuracy but long enough where I had a chance of making mistakes. I found that this boundary was the perfect zone for challenging myself accuracy-wise.

That concludes my daily typing practice to boost my typing speed up to ~130WPM. 3 warm up runs with MonkeyType. 1 test, 10 minutes practice, and another test on keybr. Do this consistently and you may see results like with me. Just remember, it’s always hardest in the beginning. It’ll get easier over time - just stick to it. You got this!
